Research date: 2026-11-23. Figures are listed latest first. "Primary" means the figure was read on the publisher's own page or release. "Press" means it came from trade press or a syndicated release that quotes the report. "Search-summary only" means it was seen only in search-engine snippets and has not been checked against a fetched page. The report writer should treat that last tier as unverified.

## Construction cost per MW of IT load (USD/W or USD/MW) by market

### Takeaway
The latest primary benchmarks are Turner & Townsend's DCCI 2026-27 (22 Oct 2026) and Cushman & Wakefield's 2026 Development Cost Guide (Aug 2026). T&T puts the US average at USD 12.08/W and Tokyo highest at USD 18.28/W. Press coverage of T&T puts the Middle East as the cheapest region, at an average of USD 9.93/W (Saudi Arabia 10.80, Qatar 9.80, UAE 9.20). C&W puts the North American all-in greenfield cost at USD 17.6M/MW, excluding IT. The three publishers measure different scopes (T&T: construction only; JLL: shell and core only; C&W: all-in including power infrastructure), so their numbers cannot be mixed in one table without a scope column.

Methodology and scope notes
- T&T DCCI 2026-27 (10th edition) covers 62 markets (12 new), 500+ live or recent projects and 401 survey respondents, with cost per watt based on "a typical 60MW design." 81% of respondents say skilled labour is constrained, and 52% saw 6-15% cost inflation in 2026 — [T&T DCCI 2026-27](https://www.turnerandtownsend.com/insights/data-centre-construction-cost-index-2026-27/)
- T&T 2025-26 edition: more than 300 projects in more than 20 countries, 52 markets — [IREI](https://irei.com/news/turner-townsen-releases-data-center-construction-cost-index/). An earlier T&T methodology describes the benchmark as an air-cooled, build-to-suit hyperscale data centre of 30-50 MW IT load — [T&T methodology page (2025)](https://reports.turnerandtownsend.com/data-centre-construction-cost-index-2025/methodology) (seen in a search summary; the 2026-27 edition changed the basis to 60 MW)
- Gulf scope caveat from press coverage: the T&T Gulf figures are for conventional air-cooled designs and exclude land, utility works and IT/chips — [Enterprise AM](https://enterpriseam.com/menaplus/2026/10/28/gulf-leads-the-world-on-data-center-construction-costs-turner-townsend-says/) (search summary)
- JLL scope caveat: the per-MW figures include only shell and core construction, and tenants typically pay for tech fit-out — [JLL 2026 Outlook](https://www.jll.com/en-us/insights/market-outlook/data-center-outlook)
- Conflict: Saudi Arabia's rank is reported as 40th of 62 in one outlet and as "23rd-cheapest" in another. Both describe the same position counted from opposite ends (62 - 40 + 1 = 23), and the USD/W value agrees — search summary of [Reuters/TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2026-10-26:newsml_Zaw6L2Cm0:0-saudi-arabia-is-costliest-gulf-market-for-data-centre-construction-turner-townsend-report/) and [Jawlah](https://jawlah.co/en/67302)
- Conflict: C&W 2026 guide release date. IREI's header says 20 Aug 2026, while one search summary said September 2026. Use August 2026 (IREI) — [IREI](https://irei.com/news/cushman-wakefield-releases-2026-data-center-development-cost-guide-citing-21-rise-in-per-mw-construction-costs/)
- AECOM Middle East Property & Construction Handbook: the 2025 edition exists (publication portal), but I found no data-centre USD/MW rate in any accessible text. The handbook directs readers to AECOM's cost consulting team for benchmarked data — [AECOM MEH portal](https://publications.aecom.com/MEH/)
- T&T also publishes a separate Middle East data-centre insight — [T&T Middle East](https://www.turnerandtownsend.com/insights/an-in-depth-look-at-data-centre-development-in-the-middle-east/) (not fetched)

### Inferences
- A USD/W figure converts to USD M/MW one-for-one (USD 12.08/W = USD 12.08M/MW). The C&W figure of 17.6M/MW is higher than T&T's 12.08 mainly because of scope: C&W is "all-in greenfield," including power infrastructure, while T&T excludes land and utility works. The gap is not a sign that either source is wrong.
- For GCC estimates, T&T's country-level figures (KSA 10.80, Qatar 9.80, UAE 9.20 USD/W) are the only published per-watt benchmarks found. They are air-cooled baselines, so a QS should add an AI/liquid-cooling premium (see the next section).

### Gaps
- No city-level figures for Riyadh, Dubai, Abu Dhabi or Doha were found, only country-level ones.
- The T&T 2026-27 GCC figures were seen only in search summaries of the press articles. The utilities-me.com article returned 403, and the T&T insight page did not list Middle East markets. Verify against the T&T interactive report before citing.
- Linesight, Mace, Arcadis and RLB data-centre cost reports were not reached within the tool budget, so I have no figures from them.
- Paywall status: the C&W full guide is gated (form download), so the figures here come from the press release. The JLL Outlook web page is free. T&T's DCCI web summary is free, but the full interactive tables may require registration.

## Cost split by discipline (MEP vs shell/civil) and AI/liquid-cooled premium

### Takeaway
Few published splits exist. C&W 2026 says power infrastructure is the single largest cost line, at 21% of a greenfield build. T&T reports an AI premium over traditional US builds of about 7-10% (2025-26) and about 10% (2026-27), with advanced AI facilities at USD 14-16/W against a USD 12.08/W US average. JLL separates shell and core (USD 11.3M/MW) from AI tech fit-out (up to USD 25M/MW).

### Cited Findings
- Power infrastructure is the single largest cost component, at 21% of a greenfield build. Switchgear costs rose about 60% between Dec 2021 and early 2026 — [C&W 2026 guide via Finviz/Business Wire](https://finviz.com/news/388803/cushman-wakefield-releases-2026-data-center-development-cost-guide-citing-21-rise-in-per-mw-construction-costs) (press; search summary)
- US data centres built for AI workloads cost about 10% more than traditional facilities. Advanced AI-specific facilities cost USD 14.00-16.00/W against the USD 12.08/W country average — [T&T DCCI 2026-27](https://www.turnerandtownsend.com/insights/data-centre-construction-cost-index-2026-27/) (primary)
- 2025-26 edition: a 7-10% construction cost premium for AI over traditional US data centres of similar IT capacity — [T&T news release 2025](https://www.turnerandtownsend.com/news/ai-data-centre-rush-raises-alarm-over-construction-supply-chain) (press summary)
- 39% of T&T 2026 respondents worked mainly on AI-ready facilities in the past 12 months, and 53% expect AI-dominant workloads in the coming year. MEP trades are cited as the most sought-after skills — [T&T DCCI 2026-27](https://www.turnerandtownsend.com/insights/data-centre-construction-cost-index-2026-27/)
- JLL: shell and core averages USD 11.3M/MW (2026F), while tenant tech fit-out for AI can reach USD 25M/MW — [JLL 2026 Outlook](https://www.jll.com/en-us/insights/market-outlook/data-center-outlook)

### Inferences
- Applying T&T's ~10% US premium to the GCC air-cooled baselines would give about USD 10.1-11.9/W for AI-ready builds in KSA, Qatar and the UAE. This is my extrapolation, not a published figure.
- The USD 14-16/W advanced-AI range implies a premium of about 16-32% over the US average, which is larger than the headline 10%.

### Gaps
- None of the accessible sources gave a full MEP vs shell/civil percentage split (mechanical %, electrical %). The C&W full guide probably contains a breakdown, but it is gated.
- No published liquid-cooling vs air-cooling cost delta per MW was found from T&T, C&W, JLL or Linesight in accessible text.

## Lead times for MV/HV transformers, generators, MV switchgear, UPS, chillers, CDUs (2024-2026)

### Takeaway
Transformers are the binding constraint. Wood Mackenzie's Q2 2025 survey puts power transformers at about 128 weeks, generator step-up (GSU) units at about 144 weeks and distribution transformers at about 30 weeks. Wood Mackenzie projected a 30% supply deficit for power transformers in 2025. C&W's 2026 guide lists generators at 60-100 weeks. Switchgear and UPS figures come mostly from secondary or contractor sources.

### Gaps

- Uptime, Schneider, Eaton and Sightline commentary on lead times was not reached within the budget.
- The switchgear, generator and UPS ranges are mostly secondary or vendor sources and differ by 2-3x across sources. Use them as directional only.

## Commissioning cost as % of MEP or construction cost

### Takeaway
The only rigorous published benchmark found is LBNL's (Mills) meta-analysis: a median of 0.4% of total construction cost (about USD 1.16/ft2) for new-construction commissioning in commercial buildings. It predates the 2024-2026 window (2009 study, 2011 paper) and is not specific to data centres. One data-centre figure (about 1.5% of construction cost) comes from a vendor-authored trade article, not a study.

### Cited Findings
- New-construction commissioning median cost is 0.4% of total construction cost (about USD 1.16/ft2) — [LBNL Cx cost-benefit page](https://cx.lbl.gov/cost-benefit.html) (primary, 2009 dataset)
- The same 0.4% figure appears in Mills (2011), *Energy Efficiency* journal — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s12053-011-9116-8) (primary, peer-reviewed, 2011)
- LBNL's 2004 assessment flags data centres among the high-tech facility types with distinct commissioning challenges, but gives no data-centre-specific percentage — [LBNL 2004 assessment](https://cx.lbl.gov/2004-assessment.html)
- About 1.5% of total construction cost is called an "ideal" commissioning budget for a complex data centre in an article by an Emerson Network Power engineer — [Data Center Knowledge](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/business/getting-the-most-out-of-commissioning-through-best-practices) (vendor opinion, undated in this search, not primary)
- ASHRAE has an AI data-centre commissioning and performance validation resource page, but no cost percentage was found on it — [ASHRAE](https://www.ashrae.org/technical-resources/ai-data-center-framework/commissioning-performance-validation)

### Inferences
- Data-centre Level 1-5 commissioning (including IST load banks, fuel and utility energy) is far more intensive than commercial-building Cx. The 0.4% LBNL median should therefore be treated as a floor, not a data-centre benchmark.

### Gaps
- No 2024-2026 primary source (BCxA, Uptime, ASHRAE, PECI) giving commissioning cost as a % of MEP or construction cost for data centres was found.
- No published split of Cx authority fees vs contractor Cx costs vs IST consumables was found.
